A 14-Day Philippines Itinerary for Two: Boracay, Palawan and Coron

By Kathy Hingan · October 19, 2026 · 11 min read
Home› Journal› A 14-Day Philippines Itinerary for Two

The couples who come to us for this journey tend to arrive with the same brief. It is their first trip to the Philippines together, sometimes their first time travelling abroad as a couple at all, and they want one seamless arrangement from the moment they land to the flight home: transfers, domestic flights, the accommodation, the boat days, and someone reachable throughout, so that the only thing left to organise on the trip is nothing at all. When they name places, they name the same two. Boracay, and Palawan.

This itinerary is our answer to that brief. Fourteen nights, five bases, one steady line north through the best water in the archipelago: Boracay for the arrival and the slowdown, Puerto Princesa for the river inside the mountain, Port Barton for the quiet, El Nido for the lagoons, and Coron for the close. It is built for two, and built so the pace never outruns the point of the trip.

How to Read This Itinerary

The route is identical at every tier: Manila, Boracay, Puerto Princesa, Port Barton, El Nido, Coron, and home. What the tiers change is not how much you do. It is how privately you do it, how much of each day is yours to waste, and where you lay your head at night.

Refined stays in established four and five-star hotels and boutique island resorts, with every island day private. Elevated moves up to five-star beachfront and private-island properties, adds private speedboats on the open-water legs, priority permits, and more meals arranged. Sovereign takes the finest villa or private-island address at every base, business class where the route offers it, a dedicated guide travelling with you the whole way, and private chef dinners on the nights that deserve one. Rest days are part of the design, not gaps in it: one open day sits inside the El Nido stretch at every tier, and the Sovereign tier holds two.

The Route at a Glance

Manila → Boracay → Puerto Princesa → Port Barton → El Nido → Coron → Manila. Fourteen nights, two guests, four flights and two private drives, one direction, no backtrack.

A private outrigger on flat turquoise water. Island days on this route are private by default, never shared.

Days 1 to 3: Boracay, and the First Sunset

You land in Manila and connect straight through to Caticlan, where a private vehicle and boat carry you across to the island. By late afternoon you are on White Beach with nothing left to arrange, and the walk along it toward the sunset is the only item on the agenda. The gentlest possible way to begin.

Boracay does one thing better than anywhere else in the country: it lets you slow down without asking permission. Two days follow with no fixed programme. Long lunches that jet lag makes necessary. The spa. On either morning, a private boat is yours if you want it, out to the quieter coves and snorkel reefs away from the main stretch, and if you would rather not move, that is also the right answer.

The tiers differ quietly here. Refined bases you at a boutique resort on the calmer side of the island. Elevated puts you at a beachfront five-star with a sunset sail on offer one evening. Sovereign takes a private pool villa on the finest sand, a dinner set on the beach on your first night, and a private chef on one evening after, cooking around what you like rather than a menu. Island hopping, as everywhere on this route, is always private and always optional.

Days 4 to 5: Puerto Princesa and the Underground River

A morning flight brings you to Puerto Princesa, the gateway to the Palawan stretch. The afternoon is deliberately soft: check in, a first taste of Palawan cooking, an early night before the centrepiece.

Day five is a private day to the Puerto Princesa Subterranean River, a UNESCO World Heritage site and one of the natural wonders of the world: a navigable river running straight through a mountain, entered by paddle boat into the dark, past cathedral-scale chambers the light never reaches. At the Elevated and Sovereign tiers, permits and timing are arranged so you enter at the first window of the day, with no queue and no crowd anywhere near you. In the afternoon, Honda Bay's islands are there for a private snorkel if the day still has energy in it. If it does not, the return is slow, and nobody minds.

The river inside the mountain, entered by paddle boat, in the dark.
A Note on Permits

Entries to the underground river are limited and timed daily. The first window of the day is booked as standard: the cave is quieter, the water is calmer, and the rest of the day stays yours.

Days 6 to 7: Port Barton, Where It Goes Quiet

A private drive north of about three hours, through rice country and forest, ends at Port Barton: a small bay with no crowds and no rush, the closest thing left to the Palawan of twenty years ago. The afternoon asks nothing of you, which in Port Barton is the entire luxury.

The second day is the reason the detour exists. A private boat, if you want one, runs out to the reefs where sea turtles feed in clear shallow water, the sandbars that appear and vanish with the tide, and the empty beaches you will likely have to yourselves, with lunch set up on the sand. At the Sovereign tier it is your own speedboat and crew, and the return is whenever the light tells you to come back. There is no headliner here, and that is the point: the hammock is a legitimate plan.

Port Barton: reefs, vanishing sandbars, and beaches you share with no one.

Days 8 to 11: El Nido and the Lagoons at the Right Hour

A shorter private drive, about two hours, brings you to El Nido and the bay that put Palawan on every map. You base yourselves on the calm edge of town: a beachfront resort on the Lio side at Refined and Elevated, open straight onto Bacuit Bay, and a private-island resort inside the bay itself at Sovereign, with the lagoons at the doorstep and the crowds on the far side of the water.

Three days here, all of them by private boat, and the timing is the whole difference. The Big and Small Lagoons in the early light or the late afternoon, when the day boats have gone and the water is yours. Hidden beaches for lunches laid out on sand you share with no one. Nacpan's long curve of sand up the coast on the slower day, and a wellness afternoon at the resort spa when the water has done its work.

You reach the lagoons in the early light, when the day boats have gone and the water is yours.

A Note on the Open Day

One of these three days is deliberately left open at every tier. No boat, no plan. It is the day the trip becomes a rest, not a tour. At the Sovereign tier a private chef dinner under the stars usually lands on it.

Days 12 to 14: Coron, Lakes Inside Islands

A brief flight across to Coron, the most dramatic water in the country and the calmest possible way to end a journey. The first afternoon is gentle: the Maquinit hot springs, warm saltwater at the edge of the sea as the light goes, and a slow first evening. If you would rather skip the flight entirely, a private expedition boat can run the El Nido to Coron leg through the islands instead: a slow, remarkable day on the water, offered at the Sovereign tier for those who want it.

The full day is a private boat through Coron's best hand: Kayangan Lake, the clearest water you will ever stand in, ringed by karst; Twin Lagoon, entered under a limestone wall at the right tide; Barracuda Lake, layered warm and cool; and the shallow Japanese shipwrecks, close enough to the surface to snorkel straight over. At the Sovereign tier the day is timed so the famous spots are empty when you reach them, with your own guide leading and the boat yours alone.

The last full day stays open. A run out to Malcapuya's white sand, a guided dive over a wreck, or a final slow morning at the resort, and then home: a private transfer and the flight to Manila to connect, with the whole return handled so the last day is as calm as the first. You leave having seen five distinct corners of one country, and none of them the crowded version.

Worth Adding: Two Ways to Mark the Milestone

A first trip abroad together only happens once, and two additions sit outside the tiers, available on all three. A private photographer, for a half day in El Nido or Coron, working quietly while you go about the day on the water: you come home with the two of you in the lagoons and on the sand, not just the scenery, and it is the single thing couples tell us afterwards they were most glad they added. Or a private dinner on the sand, a table for two on an empty beach in Boracay or El Nido, arranged discreetly and lit for the evening, with a menu built around what you love. Included already at the Sovereign tier, and easily added at either of the others.

Frequently Asked Questions

How many days do you need for Boracay and Palawan together?
Fourteen nights is the comfortable answer for a first trip covering both: two or three in Boracay to land and slow down, then the Palawan line north through Puerto Princesa, Port Barton and El Nido, and a finish in Coron. Fewer nights work if you drop a base; more reward depth over distance.
Can you fly from El Nido to Coron?
Yes. A short direct flight connects the two, about forty minutes, and it is the standard link on a northbound Palawan route. At the top tier a private expedition boat can run the same leg through the islands instead, a full day on the water rather than a flight.
How much does a private two-week Philippines itinerary for two cost?
It depends on the tier of accommodation and how much of the route is run privately, and firm figures are shared after a short call once dates are locked. What holds at every level: all in-country transport, stays, private boat days and permits are arranged as one seamless price, with only the international flights to Manila outside it.
