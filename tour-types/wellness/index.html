The Standard

Five days of
doing very little

Wellness travel fails when it is a hotel with a spa. What actually resets people is structure and subtraction: a programme designed by medical professionals, then a stretch of days where the schedule is deliberately, defensively empty.

The Philippines has both halves. The Farm at San Benito south of Manila is a genuine medical-wellness estate: vegan kitchens, live-blood analysis, colonics and counselling alongside the pools and the palms, with programmes built by doctors rather than marketers. Then the subtraction: Lio Beach in El Nido, where five days of doing very little is not laziness but the design; Port Barton, where the moon rises over an empty beach and the loudest thing is the water.

We build reset journeys as two movements: the structured programme first, while discipline is fresh, then the island days, where the only decisions are swim or read. The arc works because both halves are planned, not improvised.

Planning Notes

What to know
before you go

The Two Movements

Clinical programme first, island subtraction second. The arc matters: discipline while it is fresh, then the beach that makes it hold.

Programme Length

The Farm's reset programmes run five to fourteen days. The island days add as many as the calendar allows. Two weeks is the shape that works.

Best Season

Year-round; the Farm is climate-controlled calm. Island days are best December to May, and Port Barton is quietest outside the Philippine holiday weeks.

Medical & Dietary

The Farm handles medical clearances and dietary needs in-house. Bring records; the kitchen and clinic plan around them before arrival.

How Long

The reset that works is the one that outlasts the guilt of resting: ten to fourteen days total, with at least five unscheduled.

A Word on Screens

The island bases have wifi, and the best version of the trip turns it off. We say this in the itinerary, gently, with the honesty of people who mean it.
