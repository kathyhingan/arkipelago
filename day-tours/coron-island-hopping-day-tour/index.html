Coron, Palawan · Private full day · Your boat, your pace

Coron Island Hopping,
done properly.

Kayangan Lake at its quietest, the Twin Lagoons, a shallow wreck snorkel and a grilled lunch on a private stretch of sand, all from a boat that carries no one but you.

A whole day in the Calamianes, without a single stranger on the boat

Coron’s island-hopping day is the most copied tour in the Philippines, and the difference between versions is timing and privacy. Ours runs on a private boat with your own guide, leaves ahead of the day-trip flotilla, and keeps the schedule loose enough that a longer swim or a second pass over the reef is never a problem.

Your liaison holds the plan for the whole day: boat, permits, lunch, gear and timings, so nobody in your group is negotiating with boatmen or queueing for lake entry. You swim, eat, and move on when you feel like it.

The schedule

Six stops, one loose plan

Every private day bends a little around weather and mood. This is the shape of a normal one.

1

Morning pick-up and private boat boarding at the town quay

Your guide meets you at your hotel in Coron Town for a short briefing: the day’s route, what the water temperatures feel like, and where the dry bags live. At the quay, the boat is already loaded with gear, water and ice. We leave early on purpose, because the first hour is the difference between a lake to yourselves and a lake with a queue.

2

Kayangan Lake: the climb and the clearest water you will stand in

A short limestone stairway leads over the ridge to Kayangan, a freshwater lake held inside the island like a bowl. Your guide sets a slow pace on the climb, times your arrival against the tour boats, and shows you where the water turns from warm to cool. Life vests, goggles and a hand at the ladder are all part of the service.

Kayangan Lake · the ridge viewpoint
3

Twin Lagoons: the swim-through and the deep-water paddle

Two lagoons separated by limestone, joined by a narrow opening you swim or float through at high tide and pass by a small ladder at low tide. Your guide carries the dry bag, points out the safe line across the channel, and gives the less confident swimmers a rope to hold. Most people call this the highlight; decide for yourselves.

4

Wreck snorkel: the Lusong gunboat in shallow water

Coron sank a Japanese supply fleet in 1944, and one gunboat lies in water shallow enough to snorkel, its bow breaking the surface at low tide. Your guide tows a float, keeps the group together over the coral, and reads the current before anyone rolls in. Snorkellers of every level do this stop; it is calmer than it sounds.

Lusong gunboat · the shallow wreck snorkel
5

Beach lunch on Banul’s sand, grilled while you swim

Lunch is not a canteen stop. On a white strip of Banul Beach the crew grills fish, pork and chicken over coconut coals, with rice, salads and cut fruit under a thatched roof, set for your group only. Eat when you are hungry, swim between courses, and let the crew handle the sand in everything.

Banul Beach · the lunch stop
6

Slow afternoon reef stop, then back before golden hour

The afternoon bends around energy: a final reef stop at Siete Pecados or Coral Garden for those who want one more swim, or a sandbar drift for those who want none. The boat turns for town by late afternoon so you are back, rinsed and unhurried, with the evening free. Your liaison carries recommendations for dinner if you want them.

2

Guided throughout: a guide in the water with you at every stop, timings managed around the tour-boat waves.

3

Good to know before you go

Who this day suits: swimmers and non-swimmers alike. Every stop has a shallow ledge or a vest-supported float, and the guide adjusts the route for anyone who would rather stay on the boat.

What to bring: swimwear worn under clothes, reef-safe sunscreen, a hat, and a dry change for the ride home. Towels, water, masks and fins are on the boat.

When it runs best: the Coron season runs November to May. June to October is greener and quieter, with weather calls made day by day.

Questions

Asked before every Coron day

Is the day really private?

Yes. The boat, guide and crew are reserved for your group alone, and the route bends around your pace rather than a fixed circuit.

I am not a confident swimmer. Can I still do the lagoons?

Yes. Life vests are worn throughout, the guide carries a rope line across the channels, and the ladder route is used instead of the swim-through whenever the tide allows.

What is for lunch?

A grilled spread on Banul Beach: fresh fish, chicken and pork with rice, salads and fruit, cooked by the crew. Tell us about allergies when you book and the grill is briefed before you board.

What happens if the weather turns?

Your liaison makes the call the night before and again at the quay. If the sea is rough, the day moves to another date on your itinerary rather than running a bad one.

Can children come?

Yes. The day is private, so stops shorten or stretch around younger swimmers, and vests are sized for children.

How far in advance should we book?

In peak months, as early as you can: private boats in Coron are finite, and the best guides are booked first.
